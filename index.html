Single-use waste recycling isn't what you think it is

The problem isn't just plastic. It's single-use waste.

Recycled, composted or trashed, single-use cups and containers all start as waste we never needed to create. And every year, Americans throw billions of them away.3

But there's no such place as “away.”

It ends up in landfills, incinerators and the environment, often burdening communities that never created it.

Despite decades of recycling efforts, the EPA estimates only 8.7% of U.S. plastic waste was recycled in 2018.4

The problem is a system designed to manage waste, not prevent it.

r.World is helping change that. We've built the infrastructure to make reuse work at scale, replacing single-use waste with a system designed to use products again and again.

The future isn't better waste management. It's preventing waste in the first place. And you can help.

Several States Now Require What r.World Has Been Working On Since 2017

r.World saw it coming. r.World began building reuse infrastructure in 2017, years before the first state EPR laws passed.16 Now, the regulations are kicking in.

Seven states have passed Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) legislation as of [month 2026] (California (CA)17, Colorado (CO)18, Oregon (OR)19, Minnesota (MN)20, Maryland (MD)21, Washington (WA)22, Maine (ME)23), and EPR bills have been introduced in 12+ other states.24

Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) laws are requiring producers to pay fees based on the packaging they put into the market. California’s SB 54 requires producers to reduce single-use plastic packaging and plastic food service ware 25% by 2032, compared with 2023 levels.25 At least 10% must come from eliminating plastic components or shifting to refillable and reusable systems. Reuse is written into the statute as a compliance pathway.25 SB 54 does not require any producer or venue to use a particular reuse provider. We believe r.World's reuse system can help producers meet these targets. CalRecycle may impose administrative civil penalties of up to $50,000 per day, per violation, on noncompliant producers or producer responsibility organizations.26
